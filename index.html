For Uber drivers

Deactivated by Uber? Appeal first, then arbitrate.

If Uber deactivated your driver account, you have a right to appeal — and if the appeal is denied, you can demand arbitration. Follow these steps in order.

  1. Save your evidence. Screenshot every deactivation message and email from Uber, and keep your dashcam footage, trip logs and any messages about the trip.
  2. Appeal inside the Uber Driver app. Open the deactivation notice, tap Appeal, and submit your evidence. Uber usually decides within 7 business days, and updates appear only in the Review Center — not in support chat.
  3. If the appeal is denied, send the arbitration letter. Most first appeals are denied. The next step is a formal Notice of Dispute and Intent to Initiate Arbitration, sent by USPS Certified Mail to Uber Technologies, Inc., Attn: Legal Department – Dispute Resolution, 1515 3rd Street, San Francisco, CA 94158.
  4. Uber has 60 days to respond. Once its Legal Department receives the letter, a 60-day informal negotiation period starts, including at least one individual conference. Most cases are resolved here.
  5. If Uber does not respond, file for arbitration. File a formal Demand for Arbitration with the American Arbitration Association (AAA, adr.org) or the National Arbitration and Mediation (NAM).
